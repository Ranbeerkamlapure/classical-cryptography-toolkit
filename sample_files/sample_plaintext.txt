Design and Development of a Classical Cryptography Toolkit
Course: Network Security (Final Year ECE 2026-27)

Classical cryptography laid the foundational principles of confidentiality and obfuscation in early communications.
Substitution ciphers (Caesar, Monoalphabetic, Playfair, Hill) replace characters or blocks with alternate representations.
Polyalphabetic ciphers (Vigenere, One-Time Pad) employ multiple substitution alphabets or pseudorandom keys to flatten single-frequency analysis.
Transposition ciphers (Rail Fence, Columnar Transposition) rearrange the geometric positions of letters without altering character values.

Sample secret message:
"The security of a cryptosystem must not depend upon keeping the algorithm secret; it must depend solely on keeping the key secret." - Auguste Kerckhoffs, 1883.
